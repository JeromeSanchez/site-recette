Team coaching

My remote team can no longer get in sync

By Jérôme Sanchez, professional coach in training · Updated 24 November 2026

This page is for managers of remote, hybrid or multi-country teams who feel their team is no longer moving forward together.

“Everyone is busy, but I don't know what they're doing.” Your team works remotely, hybrid, or across several countries. You have set up sync meetings, and yet everyone heads in a different direction. The team is working, it is active, and you feel that nothing moves forward together.

You may recognise yourself

  • You find out at the last minute what has been done, or not.
  • Work gets done twice.
  • Team members don't move at the same pace, and you struggle to finish things together.
  • The sync meetings take place, but they don't align anyone.
  • The real conversations happen elsewhere: in one office, over lunch, without those who work on another site.
  • Everyone seems overloaded, so you no longer dare to hand out new work.

Questions that can help

What connects the team

  • To what extent do the members of my team share common goals?
  • What deliverables are we working on together?
  • If I asked each person what the team has to deliver in the next three months, what would they answer?

How the work is organised

  • How many topics is each person handling in parallel?
  • How does work flow between team members?
  • Who are each person's stakeholders, and what do they expect?

What gets recognised

  • What is valued and rewarded, for each person?
  • How is work assessed, individually or collectively? How does each person understand it?

How the team talks

  • What is said during our team meetings, and what is left unsaid?
  • Which emotions have a place in our conversations?
  • Where do the conversations that matter take place, and who takes part in them?

My role as a manager

  • How do I talk about each member of my team?
  • What changes in the way I behave depending on who is in front of me?
  • What information do I share, with whom, and when?
  • What do I keep control of, and what do I let go of?
  • How much do I trust the team to reach its objectives? What is that trust based on?

What coaching can bring you

You probably already have the answers. What is often missing are the questions you don't ask yourself: day to day, we all work with blinkers on. On your own, you keep circling the same questions. An outside perspective asks the ones your own answers bring to light.

The work can start with you, as a manager. Before involving the whole team, we explore together what you observe and what you already know.

It can then continue with the team. To understand how it works, I join its daily life as it is: if it works remotely, I take part remotely in its meetings. Watching the team interact reveals what no interview shows. The two approaches complement each other.

By the end, you see more clearly what is at play:

  • for you;
  • in your relationship with the team;
  • within the team;
  • between the team and its environment.

Frequently asked questions

Will a coach tell me what to do?
No. My role is to ask questions that help you see clearly. The answers are yours, and you often already know much of them.
Does the whole team need to be involved from the start?
No. We start by working with you. Extending the work to the team afterwards is not always necessary.
Can the coaching take place remotely?
Yes, in French or in English. If your team works remotely, the coaching takes place remotely, as close as possible to how the team actually works.
