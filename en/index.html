Individual coaching

Professional coaching — A confidential, neutral and structured space to help you — yourself, your managers or your staff — step back, clarify what is at stake and bring out your own solutions.

1. Navigating a new role or a transition

  • Becoming a manager for the first time : Exploring one's new professional identity, finding the right way to embody authority and defining one's own management style with former colleagues.
  • Stepping into a leadership role : Gaining perspective on one's role, developing one's strategic vision and having a space to talk in the face of leadership's isolation.
  • Going through a move or a career change : Clarifying one's deeper motivations, identifying one's resources and building one's own bearings in a new environment.

2. Developing leadership and relational stance

  • Working on interpersonal impact : Understanding how one operates, exploring how to assert oneself appropriately and adjusting one's communication in high-stakes exchanges.
  • Handling tense situations : Stepping back from one's own reactions to disagreement to find new ways of approaching conflict.
  • Changing one's relationship to delegation : Questioning one's need for control to build trust and encourage staff to take responsibility.

3. Preserving balance and managing pressure

  • Easing mental load : Clarifying priorities, questioning one's relationship to time and exploring what makes it possible to set limits and say no.
  • Taming pressure : Identifying one's personal levers to stay clear-headed and calm in the face of high targets or uncertain times.
  • Preventing burnout or preparing a return : Reflecting on one's relationship to work to redefine a healthy, sustainable pace, especially after extended leave.

4. Unblocking a complex situation and deciding

  • Breaking a deadlock : Benefiting from a non-judgemental mirror to untangle a blocked situation with a partner, a superior, a client or a peer.
  • Asserting one's legitimacy : Overcoming impostor feelings by reconnecting with one's real strengths, to dare to take one's place and own one's choices.

Team coaching

Team coaching — A safe framework that enables your team to step back from how it works, align its practices and draw on its own collective intelligence.

1. Building and bonding a team

  • Welcoming new members : Facilitating encounters, sharing perspectives and allowing the group to redefine its shared rules of the game.
  • Supporting the arrival of a new manager : Creating a space for dialogue to clarify mutual expectations and lay the foundations of a healthy working alliance.
  • Going through a merger or reorganisation : Allowing teams to express their perceptions, harmonise their practices and co-create a shared culture.

2. Easing relationships and dialogue

  • Moving beyond latent tension : Offering a regulated setting to put the unsaid into words and let the group resolve its relational blockages.
  • Breaking out of silos : Helping departments or individuals better understand each other's constraints to reinvent how they cooperate.
  • Nurturing mutual trust : Creating a space for authentic speech where everyone feels entitled to share their ideas, doubts or needs.

3. Questioning momentum and engagement

  • Finding a shared direction again : Helping a team that is running out of steam to restore meaning to its work and remobilise its energy.
  • Transforming meetings : Helping the group observe how its meetings unfold to bring out smoother ways of deciding.
  • Developing shared responsibility : Guiding the group towards greater autonomy by clarifying how roles and initiatives are shared.

4. Supporting a transformation

  • Launching a strategic project : Mobilising the group's resources to imagine solutions to an unprecedented challenge or a new activity.
  • Changing ways of working : Supporting the team's reflection and adaptation to hybrid work, new tools or agility.
  • Bouncing back after a trying period : Offering time to step back, digest a difficult stage (restructuring, overload, failure) and look ahead together.

03 · The framework

Safety and a relationship of trust.

  1. A clear coaching contract, agreed together.

  2. An explicit code of ethics: the EMCC Global Code of Ethics.

  3. Confidentiality of everything we discuss.

  4. A practice supervised by Université Paris 8.

03 · The format

How coaching unfolds.

  1. A first session to define your request, then agree together on the coaching contract and the number of sessions.

  2. Sessions of 1 h 30, around ten in general: their number depends on your goal.

  3. A training rate: half the usual price of a coaching session.

  4. The first session is included in the cost of the following sessions.

My approach

I help you identify your spheres of control and influence, and act on them.

What is professional coaching?

It means supporting people as they develop their potential and their skills, in service of professional goals. And beyond that?

A safe space to build change

Professional coaching is a safe space for an individual or a team to build the change they want to bring about.

What is at stake: bringing clarity.

  • on the client's deeper request
  • on their interactions with their system
  • on the change they want to bring about
  • on the actions they want to take

What the client takes to be the cause of what they are going through is sometimes itself an effect. The interaction between coach and client makes it possible to explore, here and now, what is producing those effects.

What coaching actually does

In a session, coach and client look together at what is happening and at what is holding the client back from reaching their goals. The coach helps bring out what goes unsaid and the pitfalls along the way: spotting what is hidden and making it visible.

It is a partnership. It helps clarify a goal, explore one's situation, develop one's resources and take action. The coach creates a space for stepping back and taking responsibility, and facilitates the client's thinking and choices.

The purpose: autonomy

The aim: to help the client become autonomous and to support their fulfilment in their working life.

For me, autonomy means:

  • a clear awareness, in touch with reality here and now
  • awareness of one's vulnerabilities, set against those of the system one works in
  • awareness of one's responsibilities and of the consequences of one's choices
  • a reflective intelligence applied to action
  • an authentic relationship with oneself and with others

The scope: working life, and personal life in its connection with professional groups.

What coaching is not

  • Management
  • Consulting
  • Passing on knowledge or experience
  • Therapy: understanding how one functions psychologically and treating psychological suffering
  • Facilitation: creating the conditions for a group to think together

The boundary with therapy deserves a closer look: coaching works in the here and now to initiate change. Psychotherapy looks for the causes of what is happening in the here and now.

My guiding question, session after session: is my intervention truly serving my client's interests?
